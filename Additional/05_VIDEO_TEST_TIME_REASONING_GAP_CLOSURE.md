# 1. Executive gap closure

## 1.1 What the supplied material already establishes

The governing protocol establishes the following architectural authorities:

1. CPCS has one universal semantic kernel; domain profiles constrain it rather than creating parallel ontologies.
2. Resolved canonical JSON owns meaning. YAML is an authoring form. XML is optional. Natural language is a provider projection.
3. Evidence, inference, authored intent, creative choice, observation, measurement, detection, interpretation, and simulation must remain distinguishable.
4. The reusable research knowledge graph, execution reasoning state, and Video Observation Graph are separate authorities.
5. The Director reasons over a distilled world/scene model.
6. Provider compilation follows capability negotiation and records loss.
7. Unknown or unsupported required controls fail closed unless an explicit degradation policy permits loss.

The target brief additionally asserts that CPCS already has “several reasoning executors,” but no implementation evidence was supplied. That assertion is treated as `authored/unverified`, not as a confirmed runtime fact.

## 1.2 What external research establishes

- Tree of Thoughts (ToT) makes the reasoning unit task-relative and combines thought decomposition, candidate generation, heuristic evaluation, and a search algorithm. It does **not** establish a universal thought size or guarantee that self-evaluation is correct. [Yao et al., NeurIPS 2023, §3](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/271db9922b8d1f4dd7aaef84ed5ac703-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Graph of Thoughts (GoT) represents thoughts as graph vertices and dependencies as edges, enabling aggregation and feedback. Its own paper deliberately leaves “thought” use-case specific. It does **not** establish that named film departments should each be autonomous agents. [Besta et al., AAAI 2024, §§1–2.1](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/29720/31236)
- Algorithm of Thoughts (AoT) means guiding a model with an algorithmic reasoning trajectory in one or a few calls. It is not the same thing as a runtime-owned external local-search service. [Sel et al., §1 and abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10379)
- Test-time compute gains depend on model capability, problem difficulty, proposer quality, verifier quality, and allocation strategy. Uniform “think longer” or uniform best-of-N is not generally compute-optimal. [Snell et al., §§1–3 and 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03314)
- Video-T1 shows that candidate search and multiple verifiers can improve sampled video generation in its tested setting, but this operates in model trajectory/frame search and does not by itself validate CPCS-level symbolic branch search. [Liu et al., §§3.1–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18942)
- VISTA provides strong direct evidence for structured temporal prompt planning, pairwise candidate selection, specialized critiques, iterative prompt refinement, preservation of a champion candidate, and bounded stopping. It also explicitly warns that automated/MLLM evaluation can be systematically biased. [Long et al., CVPR 2026, §§2 and Limitations](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Long_VISTA_A_Test-Time_Self-Improving_Video_Generation_Agent_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)
- Structured carriers affect models differently by task and model. Strict format constraints can hinder reasoning, and there is no source-supported universal winner among JSON, YAML, XML, Markdown, and natural language. [Tam et al., EMNLP Industry 2024, §§4–5](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-industry.91/); [He et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10541)

## 1.3 What is still missing

The following are genuine representation or runtime gaps, assuming they are not already present in the uninspected repository:

1. A typed, executor-relative reasoning atom with an objective atomicity test.
2. A continuity capsule with explicit sufficiency status, uncertainty, timebase, open obligations, and external-audit pointers.
3. Material-difference rules that decide whether alternatives deserve branches.
4. A typed dependency/conflict graph shared by directing views without creating parallel semantic authorities.
5. A bounded search policy with explicit operations, immutable state, rollback, stopping conditions, and multi-resource budgets.
6. A failure-localization and minimal-patch contract that protects canonical intent.
7. Separate semantic, evidence, decision, and compilation equivalence keys.
8. A difficulty/solvability-aware reasoning router calibrated on CPCS tasks.
9. Measurement records that separate detector outputs, semantic judgments, and human preferences.
10. A factorial evaluation harness that isolates reasoning mode from carrier and stochastic video generation.

## 1.4 What can be implemented now

These are low-regret, model-agnostic infrastructure:

- versioned reasoning-state schema;
- atom, capsule, branch, failure, patch, budget, and equivalence records;
- deterministic normalization and hashing;
- immutable state revisions and append-only audit events;
- hard-constraint gates, explicit loss records, and champion preservation;
- JSON Patch-style repair with precondition tests;
- instrumentation for tokens, calls, renders, verifier calls, wall time, and cost;
- a direct-mode baseline and experiment harness.

## 1.5 What requires experiments

- whether selective ToT beats direct planning on CPCS tasks;
- whether a department graph beats a single structured Director pass;
- which models benefit from AoT-like algorithmic exemplars;
- which complexity signals predict branch/search benefit;
- which verifiers correlate with human judgments for each CPCS domain;
- whether any carrier improves planning quality or provider adherence;
- equivalence tolerances for continuous time, motion, and spatial values;
- optimal render, branch, and repair budgets per provider/task family.

## 1.6 What remains unknown

- the referenced frozen package's contents;
- current CPCS executor implementation and file placement;
- current provider capability matrices and adapter versions;
- whether CPCS already has an event-sourced execution state or JSON Patch support;
- CPCS-specific calibration curves, human preference data, and failure distributions;
- whether viewer attention is measured by eye tracking, estimated by saliency, or only authored as a directing goal.

# 2. Final architecture recommendation

## 2.1 The central decision

Add **one typed execution-reasoning state and policy router**, not a collection of competing ontologies or always-on executor personas.

The runtime should expose these reasoning policies:

| Policy | Runtime meaning | Default status |
|---|---|---|
| `direct` | One candidate plan, deterministic validation, compile | Infrastructure baseline |
| `bounded_refine` | Sequentially patch a promising state | Infrastructure after patch contract |
| `selective_tree_search` | Branch only on material alternatives | Research policy behind flag |
| `typed_graph_aggregation` | Merge independently produced, typed partial decisions | Research policy behind flag |
| `bounded_local_search` | Apply a finite operator set to an immutable state | Infrastructure only where operators/verifiers exist |
| `aot_prompting` | Give a model an algorithmic search exemplar in one/few calls | Experiment-only |
| `failure_directed_repair` | Localize an observed failure and minimally patch execution controls | Priority research policy |
| `hybrid` | Router-selected composition of the above | Future, after individual calibration |

## 2.2 Concepts to reject as permanent architecture

- A universal “video atom” independent of consumer or verification boundary.
- A capsule labeled “Markovian” merely because it is short.
- Branching on superficial wording, serialization, or stylistic paraphrase.
- One LLM agent per department by default.
- A single heuristic score presented as truth.
- AoT as the name of an external search loop.
- Full prompt rewrites as the default response to localized generation failures.
- A single cache key for all kinds of equivalence.
- Token count as the only reasoning budget.
- Automatic video judges as unquestioned ground truth.

# 4. Semantic representation specification

## 4.1 Executor-relative reasoning atom

### Canonical name

`reasoning_atom`

### Definition

A `reasoning_atom` is the smallest versioned execution-reasoning unit that one named consumer can independently accept, reject, verify, replace, or depend upon without changing unrelated meaning.

### What it is not

- not necessarily one sentence, token, frame, shot, or ontology triple;
- not a copy of a full scene object;
- not a provider prompt fragment by default;
- not a reusable research concept;
- not atomic merely because it has one ID.

### Atomicity test

An object is one atom only if all conditions hold:

1. It has exactly one primary semantic purpose.
2. It has one owning executor/view.
3. It has one primary verification contract or one explicit unverified status.
4. It can be rejected or replaced without implicitly modifying another independent axis.
5. Its preconditions and postconditions can be stated without hidden prose.
6. Its semantic delta can be expressed against one or a tightly coupled set of canonical paths.

If staging and camera can vary independently, split them. If a handoff requires approach, contact, transfer, release, and reaction, represent those as composed event atoms linked by temporal and causal edges. Do not collapse them into a string.

### Atom types

| Type | Purpose | Typical owner | Canonical destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state_assertion` | Current world/character/camera/audio fact or belief | continuity projector | World state / execution state |
| `event_decision` | One authored or chosen event/phase | motion/performance/narrative | Canonical score event |
| `relation_decision` | Spatial, temporal, causal, ownership, or knowledge relation | shared semantic kernel | Canonical relation object |
| `control_decision` | Camera, performance, editing, audio, VFX, or style control | relevant directing view | Canonical score control |
| `constraint` | Hard/soft prohibition, requirement, or invariant | policy/director | Constraint set |
| `verification_condition` | Observable pass/fail or metric definition | verifier | Verification plan |
| `provider_realization` | Candidate mapping of a canonical control to a provider | compiler/adapter | Execution IR/provider plan |
| `failure_claim` | Evidence-bound claim about a failed output | evaluator | Failure record |
| `repair_operation` | Minimal proposed change to execution state | repair planner | Patch set |

### Required dimensions

```text
atom_id
atom_type
schema_version
owner
consumer
scope: project/sequence/scene/shot/event/provider-run
canonical_paths
subject_refs / object_refs
time_ref and timebase_id when temporal
coordinate_frame_id when spatial
laterality when side-specific
value
preconditions
postconditions
dependencies
conflicts

origin_class
confidence and uncertainty
evidence_refs / provenance
status
```

### Temporal and spatial semantics

- Video intervals use `[start, end)` unless an existing CPCS convention overrides it.
- `frame_index`, presentation timestamp, sample timestamp, and wall-clock reasoning time are separate.
- Temporal succession uses `precedes`; causality uses `causes` or `enables`; narrative motivation uses `motivates`. They are not aliases.
- Spatial values must reference a coordinate frame: `image_normalized`, `camera_3d`, `world_3d`, `actor_local`, or a current CPCS frame identifier.
- Side-specific actions use `left`, `right`, `bilateral`, or `unknown`; absence is not interpreted as bilateral.

### Ownership

`owner` indicates which runtime component may revise the atom. `subject_refs` identify entities affected by it. A camera atom can describe an actor but remains owned by the camera view. This prevents “mentions actor” from becoming “performance owns it.”

## 4.2 Continuity capsule

### Canonical name

`continuity_capsule`

Avoid calling it `markov_state` until sufficiency tests pass.

### Definition

A versioned, task- and executor-relative projection of all accepted history needed to choose and validate the next reasoning operation without loading the full audit trail.

### Minimum active content

| Partition | Active content | Why active |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | stable entity IDs, aliases needed now, visible traits that must persist | prevent identity drift and rebinding |
| World state | current locations, poses/states, possession, topology, material state | next actions depend on current conditions |
| Character state | physical state, active pose/action, authored knowledge, revealed knowledge, performance commitments | continuity and narrative correctness |
| Open event transactions | started-not-finished actions, contacts, transfers, entrances/exits, pending effects | avoid losing action phase |
| Camera/edit state | shot ID, framing, lens class if canonical, camera pose/motion, screen direction, continuity side, pending cut | preserve visual continuity |
| Style invariants | only enforceable and currently relevant invariants | prevent stylistic drift |
| Audio state | active dialogue, speaker, ambience/music continuity, sync obligations | audiovisual continuity |
| Narrative state | current beat, facts known to viewer/characters, unresolved reveals | prevent premature or repeated reveal |
| Viewer guidance | authored attention target; measured attention only when actual measurement exists | guide camera/edit decisions without fabricating observation |
| Constraints | active hard constraints, soft preferences, unresolved obligations | next decision validity |
| Uncertainty/belief | alternatives still plausible, confidence, unobservable fields | partial-observability honesty |
| Execution context | provider capability snapshot, adapter version, remaining budget | next operation feasibility |

### External audit memory

Keep these out of active context unless retrieved by pointer:

- full prompt and response history;
- closed events whose postconditions are already projected;
- rejected branches and verbose rationales;
- raw frame observations and full VOG history;
- superseded capsule revisions;
- all prior compiler projections;
- complete metric traces after their accepted aggregate is recorded;
- token-level reasoning text;
- source documents already distilled into accepted constraints.

The capsule retains digests and pointers to these records.

### Sufficiency status

```text
unvalidated  = fields selected by design only
provisional  = passes a declared fixture suite
validated    = passes full-history equivalence and ablation criteria for a named task/provider profile
invalidated  = a counterexample shows omitted history changes a required decision
```

Sufficiency is scoped by `executor_id`, task family, provider capability version, schema version, and tolerance profile. It is never universal.

## 4.3 Selective tree search

### Branch eligibility

A candidate deserves a distinct branch only if its normalized semantic delta changes at least one of:

- hard-constraint satisfiability;
- event order, causal dependency, contact ownership, or actor binding;
- staging topology or shot structure;
- required verification obligations;
- provider realization class (`native`, `approximate`, `semantic`, `unsupported`);
- expected compilation-loss class or severity;
- a calibrated metric by more than its declared materiality tolerance;
- a genuine creative choice the user/director has elected to compare.

Differences in whitespace, key order, prose wording, aliases, comments, audit IDs, or numerically equivalent units do not create branches.

### Branch axes

```text
staging
camera
performance
motion
editing
audio
VFX
narrative_reveal
provider_realization
```

Vary one axis at a time for controlled attribution. Permit joint-axis branches only when a dependency makes independent variation invalid; record the coupling reason.

### Candidate evaluation

Do not collapse all evidence into an unexplained scalar. Use this ordered decision procedure:

1. Reject schema-invalid or hard-constraint-invalid candidates.
2. Reject candidates with required unsupported controls unless an explicit degradation policy applies.
3. Deduplicate candidates by the applicable equivalence key.
4. Retain the Pareto frontier across required coverage, verified adherence, compilation loss, risk, and cost.
5. Use an authored or calibrated preference model only to break remaining ties.
6. Preserve uncertainty and individual metric provenance.

### Rejection codes

```text
schema_invalid
hard_constraint_failed
unsupported_required_control
equivalent_duplicate
dominated
budget_exceeded
verifier_unavailable
verification_inconclusive
causal_incoherence
temporal_incoherence
identity_violation
provider_loss_exceeds_policy
```

### Aggregation rules

1. Nodes write to canonical paths, not prose-only conclusions.
2. A shared-path collision becomes an explicit conflict record.
3. Hard constraints dominate preferences.
4. `precedes` does not imply `causes`.
5. Acyclic dependency components are topologically evaluated.
6. Cycles are permitted only for declared feedback loops and receive an iteration bound.
7. Unresolved hard conflicts block compilation.
8. Soft conflicts remain as Pareto alternatives or require an authored tie-break.
9. Aggregation produces a new accepted state revision; it does not mutate source nodes.

### When to use it

Use graph aggregation when at least two independently useful partial decisions must be merged or when cross-view constraints cannot be represented as a simple sequence. Do not invoke it for a single-shot task with no meaningful cross-view dependency.

## 4.5 Bounded local search and AoT

### Terminology correction

`aot_prompting` is an experiment in which an algorithmic exploration pattern is supplied in context. `bounded_local_search` is CPCS runtime infrastructure. They may be compared or combined, but they are not the same mechanism.

### Bounded local search contract

```text
input              accepted immutable reasoning state
focus              unsatisfied obligations + localized uncertainty
operators          finite versioned operation registry
proposal           one semantic delta per operation
verification       deterministic checks first, calibrated model checks second
selection          feasible/Pareto/champion-preserving
rollback           parent state remains immutable
stopping           success, convergence, repetition, unsolvability, or budget exhaustion
output             accepted child state or explicit no-change/failure
```

Candidate operation examples:

- split one compound event into phases;
- reorder two non-causal events;
- change shot size while preserving screen direction;
- add an explicit continuity invariant;
- replace an unsupported native control with a semantic approximation;
- increase emphasis on a failed provider clause without changing canonical intent;
- select another provider adapter;
- request targeted evidence or verification.

Each operation declares applicable atom types, touched paths, preconditions, expected postconditions, verification requirements, and worst-case cost.

### Stopping conditions

Stop when any condition holds:

- every required verification condition passes;
- no frontier candidate makes a material improvement;
- the next state repeats a decision-equivalence key;
- every remaining candidate is infeasible or dominated;
- a required value is `unknown`/`unobservable` and no authorized observation can resolve it;
- provider capability makes the task unsatisfiable;
- any resource budget is exhausted;
- iteration or wall-time deadline is reached.

## 4.6 Failure-directed refinement

### Contract

```text

→ observation records
→ verification results
→ localized failure claim
→ diagnosis with uncertainty
→ responsible canonical/execution paths
→ minimal preconditioned patch
→ invariant checks
→ recompile
→ A/B verification against preserved champion
```

### Failure classes

```text
missing_required_element
unexpected_element
identity_drift
attribute_binding_error
actor_action_binding_error
spatial_relation_error
temporal_order_error
timing_window_error
contact_error
causal_consequence_error
camera_error
edit_continuity_error
audio_presence_error
audio_sync_error
style_invariant_error
physical_commonsense_error
provider_unsupported_control
compiler_loss

```

### Localization rule

A repair may be automatic only when:

1. a failure is supported by accepted observation/evaluation evidence;
2. at least one responsible canonical or execution path is identified;
3. the diagnosis confidence meets a calibrated threshold;
4. the patch touches only permitted paths;
5. all protected invariants have executable tests;
6. the original champion remains available for comparison.

If localization is ambiguous, create competing diagnoses or request review. Do not rewrite the full prompt and call it repair.

### Patch semantics

Use ordered, versioned patch operations equivalent to RFC 6902. Start patches with `test` operations against the source revision and protected values. A failed `test` aborts the patch; it must not silently target a newer state.

The canonical target is not changed merely because the generated video missed it. Repairs normally adjust execution controls, decomposition, emphasis, provider routing, or adapter strategy. Changing authored intent requires separate authorization.

### Initial deterministic router

| Observable state | Initial mode | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| No material choice, all hard controls expressible, no prior failure | `direct` | Search has no identified value |
| One promising state with a localized, patchable weakness | `bounded_refine` | Sequential exploitation |
| Two or more material alternatives on one/few axes | `selective_tree_search` | Controlled exploration |
| Independently produced partial decisions with cross-dependencies/conflicts | `typed_graph_aggregation` | Merge and conflict handling |
| Finite operators and executable verifiers exist | `bounded_local_search` | Search is bounded and testable |
| A generated artifact has a localized failure | `failure_directed_repair` | Minimal counterexample-driven correction |
| Verifier coverage is insufficient or task appears unsatisfiable | stop/review/provider switch | More sampling is not justified |

Thresholds such as “two branches” are initial engineering rules, not scientific truths. Log them and replace them only with held-out CPCS evidence.

## 4.8 State equivalence

There is no single safe equivalence relation.

| Key | Preserves | May ignore | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `byte_key` | Exact bytes | Nothing | Artifact identity/debug |
| `structural_key` | Parsed structure after format canonicalization | Whitespace, object-key order | Round-trip/serialization checks |
| `semantic_key` | Resolved canonical meaning | Carrier, comments, aliases, audit timestamps, unordered-set order | Branch dedup/experiment matching |
| `evidence_key` | Meaning plus evidence/provenance state | Presentation noise only | Evidence-sensitive retrieval/audit |
| `decision_key` | Enabled operations, hard constraints, unresolved obligations, current world projection | Closed audit history | Search cache/reuse |
| `compile_key` | Semantic state plus provider capability/adapter/compiler versions | Non-provider audit fields | Provider compilation cache |

### Normalization algorithm

1. Validate against the declared schema version.
2. Resolve defaults and inheritance into canonical JSON.
3. Resolve aliases to stable IDs.
4. Convert units to declared base units.
5. Preserve ordered arrays; sort only arrays declared as sets.
6. Normalize interval convention and coordinate-frame identifiers.
7. Preserve `unknown`, `unobservable`, absent, and explicit `null` as distinct states.
8. Remove fields listed by the named equivalence profile only.
9. Apply tolerance quantization only where the profile defines a valid domain tolerance.
10. Canonicalize the semantic projection with RFC 8785/JCS.
11. Hash with a versioned algorithm such as SHA-256.
12. Include schema version, equivalence-profile version, and relevant capability/compiler versions in the hash preimage.

RFC 8785 sorts object properties recursively but preserves array order. Therefore JCS alone is insufficient for semantic equivalence; CPCS must first normalize units, defaults, IDs, set-like arrays, and non-semantic fields.

## 4.9 Authority, lifecycle, and composition summary

| Object | Authority location | Preconditions | Postconditions | Key dependencies | Must not become |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `reasoning_atom` | Execution reasoning state; accepted value may map into canonical score | valid owner, consumer, paths, origin, and applicable frames/timebase | accepted/rejected/superseded with verification state | existing semantic schemas and validators | reusable research-KG fact by default |
| `continuity_capsule` | Distilled world + execution state | accepted prior state and projection policy | versioned active projection with sufficiency status | identity/world/event/camera/audio/narrative state | full audit log or raw VOG |
| `branch_candidate` | Execution search state | material semantic delta from immutable parent | selected, frontier, rejected, or deduplicated branch | equivalence profile, hard constraints, capability snapshot | new ontology/profile |
| `reasoning_dependency_graph` | Execution reasoning graph | typed node refs and relations | conflict-resolved accepted state or explicit block | atoms, constraints, verifier results | research KG or VOG |
| `reasoning_budget` | Orchestrator/execution policy | configured limits and instrumentation | consumed vector plus explicit stop reason | router policy, provider/render costs | token-only heuristic score |
| `failure_record` | Evaluation/execution audit; evidence remains in VOG/measurement store | accepted target plus observed/generated artifact evidence | localized/ambiguous/unlocalized diagnosis state | VOG, detector/metric versions, target refs | observation rewritten as canonical truth |
| `repair_plan` | Execution IR | localized failure, permitted paths, protected invariants, source revision | validated child state or rollback | patch engine, compiler, verifiers | silent full prompt rewrite |
| equivalence keys | Derived runtime metadata | valid normalized state and named profile | cache/dedup key scoped to its consumer | schema, tolerance, capability, adapter versions | one universal identity key |

Research evidence remains in the reusable research knowledge layer. It can justify a Director decision, but a retrieved paper claim does not directly become world state, score, or provider control. Generated video observations remain in the VOG/evaluation layer and can trigger execution repair; they do not overwrite authored intent.

## 5.1 Universal measurement record

Measurement records must not hide a semantic judgment behind a numeric field. A VLM saying “the action looks correct” is `interpreted` or `detected`, not a physical measurement.

## 5.2 Video-target metrics

| Metric | What is measured | Source | Units/aggregation | Missing/occlusion/camera rules | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `required_element_recall` | Required canonical elements detected in output | object/action/audio detectors plus reviewed mapping | fraction per requirement class | unobservable elements excluded only with reason; detector miss remains uncertainty | measurable with detector calibration |
| `unexpected_element_rate` | Elements violating explicit negative constraints | detectors/VLM plus constraint mapping | violations per video | unknown if detector lacks class coverage | partly detected/interpreted |
| `temporal_order_accuracy` | Whether required partial-order edges hold | event intervals from VOG | correct edges / observable edges | overlapping uncertain intervals yield `inconclusive`; camera cuts do not reorder world events | measurable after event detection |
| `interval_iou` | Overlap between target and observed event interval | timestamps | intersection/union | requires same timebase; missing endpoint is unknown | measurable |
| `contact_time_error` | Difference between target and observed contact onset | contact detector/tracker | milliseconds or frames; median/95th percentile | occluded contact is unobservable; do not interpolate unless model declared | measurable where contact visible |
| `spatial_relation_accuracy` | Whether target relation holds over required interval | detection/tracking/depth | frame-weighted accuracy | require coordinate frame; compensate camera only with valid transform | measurable with uncertainty |
| `identity_switch_rate` | Track identity changes not supported by scene logic | tracker/face/entity embeddings | switches per entity-minute | occlusion gaps handled by tracker confidence and re-ID policy | measurable but detector-dependent |
| `attribute_binding_accuracy` | Correct attribute attached to correct entity over time | detection/tracking/VLM | correct bindings / observable bindings | unknown when entity or attribute unobservable | detected/interpreted |
| `action_binding_accuracy` | Correct actor performs required action | action detection/tracking | correct bindings / requirements | actor occlusion yields unknown, not automatic fail | detected/interpreted |
| `camera_trajectory_error` | Difference between target and recovered camera path | calibrated pose/SLAM | translational and angular error | invalid without recoverable geometry/scale; moving objects must be masked | measurable only in suitable scenes |
| `camera_semantic_adherence` | Pan/tilt/dolly/orbit/framing class matches target | camera classifier/VLM | categorical accuracy | use when metric trajectory is unavailable; do not report as pose error | detected/interpreted |
| `audio_sync_offset` | Difference between audio event and target/visual event | audio event timestamps + visual events | milliseconds; signed offset | unknown if event onset cannot be localized | measurable |
| `physical_commonsense` | Whether depicted dynamics appear physically plausible | human labels or calibrated evaluator | binary/ordinal with agreement | separate from caption adherence; not exact physical simulation | interpreted; human preferred for benchmark |
| `semantic_adherence` | Whether entities/actions/relations satisfy target | human/VLM/detectors | per-requirement vector then aggregate | preserve evaluator confidence and coverage | interpreted/detected |

VideoPhy explicitly separates semantic adherence from physical commonsense and reports inter-annotator variation; CPCS should preserve those separate verdicts rather than compute one opaque “quality” number. [VideoPhy, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03520)

T2V-CompBench shows why binding, spatial, motion, action, interaction, and numeracy checks should remain separate. [T2V-CompBench, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14505)

| Metric | Definition | Source | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|

| `repair_regression_rate` | previously passing protected invariants that fail after repair | A/B verifier | fraction |
| `verifier_human_agreement` | agreement/correlation with blinded human labels | evaluation study | kappa/correlation with CI |
| `verifier_calibration` | predicted confidence vs empirical correctness | held-out labels | Brier score/ECE |
| `state_reuse_hit_rate` | valid cache hits under declared equivalence profile | runtime | fraction |

## 5.4 Semantics that must not be mislabeled as measurements

- `viewer_attention_target` is authored/creative unless eye-tracking measures an actual viewer.
- saliency estimation is a detected proxy, not human attention.
- `narrative_knowledge` is authored/derived from reveal events; it is not visually measured in ordinary generated video.
- mood, engagement, aesthetics, emotional impact, and performance quality are interpreted judgments unless a specific instrument and construct-validity study is declared.
- causal correctness cannot be inferred merely from event succession.
- camera trajectory is unobservable from some clips; a semantic camera label is not a metric pose.

- `format: date-time` is an annotation unless the selected validator is configured to assert it; validator behavior must be fixed in the runtime contract.
- `value` remains open here because it must reference existing CPCS semantic definitions. Do not duplicate those definitions inside the reasoning schema.

- The patch definition is abbreviated. The production validator must enforce RFC 6902 operation-specific requirements: `add`, `replace`, and `test` require `value`; `move` and `copy` require `from`; every `path`/`from` is a valid JSON Pointer.
- Real implementations should replace generic object placeholders with `$ref` links to existing CPCS schemas.

# 7. Representation equivalence and loss

## 7.1 Same meaning across carriers

```text
Authored YAML
  declares human-friendly event, timing, laterality, camera, audio, and invariants
        ↓ resolution
Canonical JSON
  expands IDs, defaults, timebase, interval convention, provenance, constraints,
  verification records, budget, state revision, and equivalence profiles
        ↓ optional projection
XML
  preserves ordered namespaced event envelope but may omit execution/audit detail
        ↓ provider compilation
Natural language
  describes visible actions and controls in provider-compatible wording
```

## 7.2 Equivalence table

| Semantic element | YAML | Canonical JSON | XML | Natural language | Expected loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stable IDs | explicit but author-friendly | authoritative | explicit attributes | usually omitted | NL cannot reliably round-trip IDs |
| Left hand | `effector: left_hand` | explicit laterality and entity ref | `effector="left_hand"` | “with her left hand” | NL may be ignored by provider |
| Contact interval | two-number author field | typed interval + timebase + convention | start/end attributes | “around 2.3–2.5 seconds” | provider precision may be unenforceable |
| Event order | list order plus phases | explicit order/temporal edges | ordered elements | discourse order | NL order can be interpreted loosely |
| Causality | explicit relation if authored | typed edge | explicit element/attribute if projected | connective phrase | prose can conflate order and cause |
| Provenance | compact or inherited | authoritative | optional namespace | omitted | expected representation loss |
| Uncertainty | author can state unknown | explicit typed state | possible but verbose | often omitted | omission can create false certainty |
| Budget | optional author config | authoritative execution object | normally omitted | omitted | not provider-facing |
| Audit identity | omitted | pointers | omitted | omitted | retained externally |

## 7.3 Round-trip rule

Only YAML ↔ resolved JSON and JSON ↔ XML adapters with explicit schemas are candidates for semantic round-trip tests. Natural language is evaluated by field preservation and provider adherence, not exact round-trip equality.

# 8. Compiler semantics

## 8.1 Required flow

```text
retrieved research evidence
→ Director interprets evidence for current task
→ reasoning atom(s)
→ accepted canonical score/control
→ control candidate
→ provider capability snapshot
→ mapping classification
→ provider projection
→ compilation-loss record
→ generation
→ VOG/measurement/interpretation
→ verification
→ accept, branch, repair, reroute, or stop
```

## 8.2 Mapping classes

| Class | Meaning | Required record |
|---|---|---|
| `native` | Provider exposes a direct, versioned control with compatible semantics | native parameter, adapter version |
| `approximate` | Provider exposes a related control but units/range/meaning differ | transform, bounds, expected error |
| `semantic` | Only natural-language or conditioning language can request the behavior | emitted clause, uncertainty, no false precision |
| `unsupported` | No credible control path exists | blocking/degradation decision |

## 8.3 Example of inexpressible precision

Canonical requirement:

```json
{
  "event": "glass_table_contact",
  "time_window_s": [2.3, 2.5],
  "required": true,
  "tolerance_ms": 100
}
```

If a provider accepts only free-form prompting and exposes no event-time or trajectory control, the compiler may emit “contact around 2.4 seconds,” but the classification is `semantic`, not `native` or `approximate`. The loss record should contain:

```json
{
  "canonical_path": "/score/events/evt_place_glass/contact/time_ref",
  "mapping_class": "semantic",
  "loss_code": "temporal_precision_unenforceable",
  "required": true,
  "requested_tolerance_ms": 100,
  "provider_guarantee": "none",
  "policy_decision": "block_or_explicit_degrade"
}
```

No prompt wording converts an unenforceable semantic request into an exact control.

## 8.4 Search and compilation interaction

- Branches must be scored after capability negotiation when provider realization differs.
- Two semantically identical plans can have different `compile_key` values under different provider profiles.
- A provider-only wording alternative is not a semantic branch; it is a realization candidate under the same semantic state.
- Compilation loss is part of candidate feasibility and Pareto comparison.
- Provider outputs never overwrite canonical intent. They create observed/simulated execution records.

# 9. Model-conditioning and data-language effect

## 9.1 Evidence-based conclusion

There is no defensible universal claim that JSON, YAML, XML, Markdown, natural language, or a hybrid is always best for reasoning or adherence.

The most relevant controlled evidence reports:

- strict structured-output constraints can degrade reasoning-intensive tasks;
- output key order can alter whether a model reasons before answering;
- classification tasks can benefit from a constrained answer space;
- format sensitivity varies by model;
- parsing failure does not explain every accuracy difference;
- generating/reasoning in natural language and converting afterward can separate semantic work from serialization, though conversion can introduce its own errors.

See [Tam et al., §§4–5](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-industry.91/) and [He et al., experimental results](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10541).

## 9.2 CPCS representation policy

- YAML: authored intent, inheritance, profiles, comments, human editing.
- JSON: resolved canonical meaning, validation, hashing, diffing, API contracts.
- XML: only for ordered/namespaced/mixed-content envelopes or provider interfaces that demonstrate a benefit.
- Natural language: reasoning scratch output where allowed and provider-facing compilation.
- Hybrid: concise natural-language task plus machine-readable canonical references/constraints; test rather than assume.

Do not force private reasoning traces into canonical schemas. Require structured decisions, evidence, uncertainty, and verification records—the products of reasoning—not hidden token-level rationales.

## 9.3 Controlled carrier experiment

### Independent variables

1. Carrier: `NL`, `YAML`, `JSON`, `XML`, `hybrid`.
2. Reasoning mode: `direct`, `aot_prompting`, `selective_tree_search`, `typed_graph_aggregation`, `failure_directed_repair`.
3. Task block: simple, temporal, causal, multi-actor, cross-view conflict, provider-loss, repair.
4. Model/provider family, analyzed separately.

### Controls

- one frozen canonical semantic task per fixture;
- mechanically generated carrier projections from the same task;
- identical field order where the carrier permits;
- identical instructions and examples except syntax-required changes;
- fixed decoding settings and seeds when supported;
- same context evidence;
- same output schema and verifier versions;
- same compute budget per comparison;
- no format-specific extra semantic hints;
- randomize presentation order;
- separate planning-only tests from video-render tests.

### Outcomes

```text
schema validity
semantic field preservation
hard-constraint pass rate
left/right preservation
temporal-order preservation
causal-edge preservation
omission rate
contradiction rate
malformed syntax rate
input/output tokens
latency
model calls
provider adherence after render
```

### Analysis

Report cell-level results, confidence intervals, effect sizes, and carrier × reasoning-mode × model interactions. Do not pool models into a universal winner. Use a predeclared missing/failure policy. A carrier becomes a provider/profile default only after held-out replication.

# 10. Reasoning-mode experiments

## 10.1 Two-stage evaluation design

### Stage A — planning and compiler isolation

No video generation. Evaluate whether each mode produces valid, semantically correct, non-contradictory canonical state and provider projection. This isolates reasoning and formatting from stochastic rendering.

### Stage B — generation and repair

Generate provider outputs from accepted plans. Evaluate provider adherence, failure localization, repair scope, regression, renders, and cost. The same accepted canonical task is used across modes.

## 10.2 Fixture families

| Fixture | Required stress |
|---|---|
| F1 simple direct | one actor, one action, no material branch |
| F2 laterality | left/right action where reversal is a hard failure |
| F3 contact phases | approach → contact → transfer/release → effect |
| F4 two-actor binding | each actor has a distinct action/attribute |
| F5 spatial topology | relative placement changes across time |
| F6 causal consequence | effect must follow and depend on contact/action |
| F7 camera-performance conflict | desired framing conflicts with visibility of required action |
| F8 narrative reveal | viewer and character knowledge must remain distinct |
| F9 audio sync | dialogue or sound must align to a visible event |
| F10 provider gap | one required control is semantic/unsupported |
| F11 localized failure | injected or observed single-path failure suitable for patch |
| F12 ambiguous failure | evidence supports multiple diagnoses |
| F13 equivalent carriers | multiple serializations of identical meaning |
| F14 misleading similarity | similar wording but materially different actor/order/control |

## 10.3 Reasoning modes

Do not use `AoT` as a synonym for local search in the experiment table.

| Label | Exact implementation |
|---|---|
| `direct` | one generation; deterministic validation; no revision |
| `aot_prompting` | one/few calls with a frozen algorithmic search exemplar |
| `selective_tree_search` | material branch generator + evaluator + bounded search |
| `typed_graph_aggregation` | independent typed proposals merged through graph rules |
| `bounded_local_search` | fixed operation registry and immutable state transitions |
| `failure_directed_repair` | render/observation → localized patch → recompile |
| `hybrid` | predeclared router, not a hand-selected best result after the fact |

## 10.4 Budget levels

Define budgets as vectors, not only token counts. Example experimental tiers:

```text
B0 direct: 1 model call, 0 extra verifier calls, 1 render
B1 light:  up to 3 model calls, 2 candidates, 1–2 renders
B2 medium: up to 8 model calls, 4 candidates, 3–4 renders
B3 high:   predeclared upper bound, used only on hard/solvable fixtures
```

Exact values are experimental configuration. Log actual tokens, verifier calls, renders, GPU time, wall time, and money.

## 10.5 Randomization and replication

- Use at least multiple independent runs per stochastic cell; determine count by pilot variance/power rather than an arbitrary universal number.
- Block by canonical task and provider/model.
- Preserve all failed and invalid runs; do not rerun only failures.
- Blind human pairwise comparisons to mode, carrier, and provider prompt.
- Include the direct result as a champion in iterative comparisons.
- Freeze verifier and adapter versions within a study.
- Separate development fixtures from held-out certification fixtures.

## 10.6 Adoption criteria

A reasoning policy may become a default only if, on held-out tasks:

1. it improves at least one predeclared primary quality/adherence outcome;
2. it does not worsen hard-constraint violations beyond the declared non-inferiority bound;
3. its improvement persists under complete cost accounting;
4. verifier gains correlate with blinded human evaluation where judgments are subjective;
5. failures, malformed states, and unsupported controls remain visible;
6. the result replicates across the intended provider/model profile.

If a mode improves an automatic judge but not human or executable checks, it is not validated.

# 11. Verification requirements

## 11.1 Schema and representation

- Draft/dialect is pinned.
- Every instance validates.
- Unknown fields fail at closed leaves.
- YAML resolves deterministically to canonical JSON.
- XML projection validates against its schema if XML is enabled.
- Natural-language projection records non-round-trippable fields.
- Semantic hashes remain stable under whitespace, key-order, and allowed set-order changes.
- Semantic hashes change when actor binding, laterality, event order, causality, required exactness, or provider-relevant meaning changes.

## 11.2 Atom

- exactly one owner and consumer;
- at least one canonical path;
- no undeclared side-specific omission;
- timebase required for temporal values;
- coordinate frame required for spatial numeric values;
- causal edges cannot be auto-created from sequence alone;
- atom split fixture catches independently variable axes.

## 11.3 Capsule

- projection can reconstruct all active hard constraints;
- open event transactions retain phase and ownership;
- full-history vs capsule continuation produces identical hard-validity decisions on certification fixtures;
- field ablation identifies which omissions change decisions;
- closed audit records are retrievable by pointer;
- `validated` is rejected without a matching validation profile/version.

## 11.4 Search/graph

- equivalent candidates deduplicate;
- material differences survive;
- hard-invalid candidates never win on preference score;
- Pareto selection is reproducible;
- graph collisions produce explicit conflicts;
- undeclared cycles fail;
- declared feedback cycles stop at their bound;
- parent/champion state remains immutable.

## 11.5 Failure and repair

- every failure references observation/evaluation evidence;
- automatic repair requires localization;
- every patch begins with source/protected-value tests;
- patch touches only allowed paths;
- canonical authored intent remains unchanged without authorization;
- all protected invariants rerun;
- no regression is silently accepted;
- rollback restores the exact parent state;
- repeated equivalent repair stops the loop.

## 11.6 Budget/router

- all resource dimensions are recorded;
- no mode starts when its minimum feasible budget is unavailable;
- budget exhaustion returns an explicit state;
- router decisions include input features and policy version;
- routing thresholds are configuration, logged and testable;
- held-out calibration is required before dynamic compute claims.

- hard-constraint pass/fail;
- semantic field preservation;
- temporal/spatial/causal correctness;
- binding and identity correctness;
- compilation loss;
- branch materiality/deduplication;
- patch scope and repair regression;
- verifier calibration/human agreement;
- full resource cost.

## 13.5 Future research

- calibrated CPCS difficulty and solvability models;
- provider-specific control reliability, not only capability presence;
- causal and physical verification beyond semantic VLM judgment;
- uncertainty propagation from VOG observations into repair decisions;
- long-horizon capsule sufficiency across scenes/edits;
- human preference models that preserve domain/cultural specificity;
- active selection of when human review is worth its cost.
