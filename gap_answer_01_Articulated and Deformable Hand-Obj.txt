Articulated and Deformable Hand-Object State Transitions for Generative Video: Representation, Causal Compilation, and Verification in CPCSBLUF and FindingsGenerative video diffusion architectures frequently fail when synthesizing unobserved, physically complex state transitions involving articulated and deformable objects. In the benchmark scenario of a person interacting with a sling bag—where a reference video cuts directly from a zipped closure to an already open interior—generative models struggle to invent the unobserved physical transition. Typical failure modes include hand contact drift, failure of closure seams to unlatch, visual stretching of fabric panels without topological separation, and incoherent emergence of interior cavities. These breakdowns occur because standard image and video diffusion models operate primarily on visual-texture dynamics rather than structured physical, kinematic, and topological state representations.To resolve these systematic failures, the Canonical Physical Control Framework (CPCS) must transition from unstructured text-to-video generation to a causal, multi-tiered state compilation pipeline. The core findings and architectural requirements identified in this research report are:Typed Object-Part Representation: Deformable and articulated objects cannot be treated as single rigid meshes or uniform latent vectors. CPCS must represent objects as Kinematic and Deformable Part Trees, integrating rigid nodes, jointed links (revolute and prismatic), deformable fabric boundaries, and state-dependent topological closure constraints such as zipper seams and buttons.Causal Transition Compilation: Unobserved transitions caused by hard video cuts must be compiled into deterministic causal sequences using domain planning formalisms such as the Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL). When a reference video omits intermediate motion, CPCS must interpolate the missing causal states—slider displacement, seam release, hand grip transfer, panel separation, and cavity volume expansion—rather than permitting the diffusion model to invent continuous pixel blending.Bounded Bimanual Contact Fields: Monocular video cannot directly measure tactile force or exact internal bone configurations. CPCS must anchor hand control to parametric models such as MANO and enforce dynamic contact fields. Contact optimization must respect soft-tissue deformation boundaries, specifically a maximum allowable penetration threshold of 2 mm for finger pads under standard grip forces, while tracking contact identity across occlusions.Multi-Carrier Execution Selection: Text prompts are fundamentally incapable of communicating exact spatial trajectories, structural topology splits, and multi-finger grasp forces. CPCS must dynamically select dense execution carriers—such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) mobility fields, dense depth streams, Plücker camera and trajectory embeddings, and sub-part segmentation tracks—based on provider capability negotiation.Topological Verification Metrics: Plausibility checks must be replaced by explicit observable physical verification, measuring metric seam separation, cavity visibility ratios, interpenetration depth bounds, and kinematic Chamfer distances.Source and Evidence RegistryThe following registry documents the primary research sources, official benchmarks, datasets, and domain models establishing the evidence base for CPCS state transition representation, contact dynamics, and carrier control.Source IdentityURL / DOILocator / SectionSupporting Passage SummaryEvidence ClassLimitationsCPCS Concept AffectedARCTIC[cite: 1, 9]https://arctic.is.tue.mpg.de/ / 10.1109/cvpr52729.2023.01244CVPR 2023, Sec. 1–3, Fig. 1Provides 2.1M video frames with ground-truth 3D hand/object meshes and dynamic contact information for bimanual articulated manipulation.Measured / BenchmarkCaptured in controlled MoCap lab; limited to 11 articulated object categories.interaction lifecycle, contact identity across occlusionContactPose / ContactOpt[cite: 7, 11]https://www.pgrady.net/contactopt/ContactOpt.pdfECCV 2020 / CVPR 2021, Sec. 1–3Captures thermal contact maps; defines DiffContact allowing up to 2 mm soft tissue interpenetration for ideal finger pad contact.Measured / ObservedThermal residual decay limits fast sequential contacts; static object focus.object affordance constraints, action preconditions and effectsPartNet-Mobility / SfA[cite: 4, 5, 16]10.1109/LRA.2023.3283221 / RSS 2020RSS 2020 / IROS 2023, Sec. CPCS replaces visual morphing with a formal causal action network grounded in Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) semantics.Causal Sequence for Hand-Driven Object OpeningThe full causal sequence for opening a closed container progresses through six distinct physical states connected by five explicit hand-driven actions:State 0 (Fully Closed): The closure seam is fully interlocked ($\sigma = 0$), the slider is at the start position ($s = 0$), the front panel is locked flush against the rear shell, side gussets are fully compressed, and cavity visibility is zero.Transition 1 (Zipper Displacement): Action unzip_closure is executed. The left hand holds the top handle or rear shell to provide stabilizing counter-force. The right hand establishes a pinch grip on the zipper slider tab and applies force along the prismatic path, displacing the slider from $s = 0$ to $s = 1$.State 1 (Seam Released): The closure seam unlatches ($\sigma = 1$). The topological seam constraint between the front panel and rear shell is removed, granting the front panel independent rotational and flexural DoFs.Transition 2 (Contact Transfer): Action transfer_manipulation_contact is executed. The right hand releases the zipper slider tab and moves to establish a stable precision or palm grasp on the upper edge of the freed front panel.State 2 (Panel Grasped): Hand contact ownership shifts to the front panel while the left hand maintains structural support on the rear shell.Transition 3 (Panel Separation and Folding): Action separate_and_fold_panel is executed. The right hand pulls the front panel away from the rear shell, causing angular hinge rotation and flexural bending along the panel's lower boundary.State 3 (Gussets Expanded): As the panel displaces beyond a threshold distance, the tensile pull forces the side gussets to un-fold and expand from a compressed state to an expanded state.Transition 4 (Cavity Exposure): Action reveal_interior_cavity occurs as a direct geometric effect of panel separation and gusset expansion, unoccluding the spatial volume relative to the viewing camera.State 4 (Stable Open State): The front panel reaches its maximum open fold position, the side gussets are expanded, the interior cavity achieves maximum visibility ($V_{cavity} \ge 0.70$), and hand contacts remain stable or cleanly release.Formal Action DefinitionsThe formal mechanics, preconditions, effects, and failure modes of the primary actions in this sequence are defined as follows:Action 1: unzip_closurePreconditions: closure_seam is locked ($\sigma = 0$); hand_left maintains stabilizing contact on rear_shell or top_handle; hand_right maintains pinch contact on zipper_slider.Dynamics: hand_right exerts pulling force along prismatic seam vector $v$, displacing zipper_slider position $s$ along $[0, 1]$.Effects: closure_seam interlock ratio transitions to released ($\sigma = 1$); seam constraint unlatches; front_panel gains spatial movement DoFs.Failure States: Slider detachment (hand_right breaks contact before $s = 1$); jammed seam (slider moves but $\sigma$ remains $0$, causing fabric stretching).Action 2: transfer_manipulation_contactPreconditions: closure_seam is released ($\sigma = 1$); internal textures and persistent contents become visible.Failure States: Incoherent cavity (interior flickers, dissolves, or fails to expand).Synthesis of Unobserved Transitions Across Hard CutsWhen a reference video presents a hard cut from a closed bag directly to an open bag, the generator receives zero optical flow or frame guidance for the intermediate transition. In this scenario, the reference video explicitly skips Transition 1 (unzip_closure), Transition 2 (transfer_manipulation_contact), and Transition 3 (separate_and_fold_panel). Asking a generative model to infer this jump in a single step causes latent diffusion blending, resulting in hand drift and panel stretching.CPCS resolves hard cuts through an Unobserved Transition Interpolator. The system extracts initial closed state $S_0$ from the pre-cut reference frames and target open state $S_4$ from the post-cut reference frames. Identifying that $S_0 \to S_4$ violates continuous physical transition constraints, CPCS automatically expands the causal graph and inserts the missing intermediate states ($S_1, S_2, S_3$). Instead of requesting an end-to-end video generation directly from text, CPCS compiles explicit structural guides—including depth sequences, part segmentation masks, and MANO hand pose keyframes—for each interpolated intermediate state. The video generation engine is then executed over sub-sequences ($S_0 \to S_1 \to S_2 \to S_3 \to S_4$), guaranteeing topological continuity across the omitted transition.Hand-Contact and Occlusion RepresentationAccurate representation of bimanual hand-object state transitions requires balancing kinematic expressiveness against the physical ambiguity inherent in monocular video inputs.Epistemic Categorization of Hand-Object DataTo avoid over-claiming measurements that cannot be recovered from monocular video feeds, CPCS explicitly categorizes all hand-object variables into four distinct epistemic classes:Observed Variables: Direct 2D image-space data extracted without structural assumptions, including RGB pixel arrays, 2D hand bounding boxes, and multi-view video feeds.Detected / Estimated Variables: High-dimensional parameters recovered via deep pose estimation models, including 3D MANO hand joint coordinates ($21\text{ joints} \times 3\text{ DoF}$), 3D object bounding volumes, and camera extrinsics.Measured / Inferred Physical Variables: Contact maps and force distributions derived either from calibrated multi-view MoCap/thermal rigs (such as ARCTIC or ContactPose) or optimization layers (such as DiffContact). In monocular pipelines, these are class-labeled as Inferred Physical Contacts.Authored / Rule-Based Constraints: Domain specifications, including PDDL preconditions, PartNet-Mobility joint boundaries, and target carrier configuration rules.Contact Field Physics and Deformable Soft-Tissue BoundsModeling hand-object interaction as rigid point constraints produces visual chatter and mesh interpenetration in generative outputs. Human skin undergoes non-rigid deformation during contact: finger pads deform by $2\text{ to }3\text{ mm}$ under standard contact forces ($\approx 1\text{ N}$), while palm tissue deforms up to $5\text{ mm}$.CPCS adopts the differentiable penetration model established by DiffContact, defining an allowable soft-tissue interpenetration zone where mesh overlap $d \le 2.0\text{ mm}$ for finger phalanges and $d \le 5.0\text{ mm}$ for palm vertices is treated as ideal contact. Penetration beyond these thresholds is penalised using a one-sided loss function:$$\mathcal{L}_{contact} = \sum_{v \in V_{hand}} \max(0, d(v, M_{object}) - d_{threshold})$$where $d(v, M_{object})$ represents the distance from hand vertex $v$ to object surface $M_{object}$, and $d_{threshold}$ is set to $2.0\text{ mm}$ for fingers and $5.0\text{ mm}$ for palm surfaces.Bimanual Occlusion and Tracking TopologyBimanual manipulation of flexible containers involves frequent self-occlusion and contact transfer. proposed framework establishes a structured architecture for articulated state transitions, several theoretical contradictions, empirical limitations, and unresolved research challenges remain.Theoretical and Engineering ContradictionsLatent Smoothing vs. Topological Discontinuity: Video diffusion models are inherently optimized to maintain smooth spatiotemporal continuity across latent features. However, topological unlatching (such as zipper release, fabric tearing, or button unfastening) represents a step-function discontinuity in the object's mesh graph. Forcing a continuous latent diffusion process to execute a sharp topological graph split often causes the model to default to smooth elastic visual stretching (FAIL-02).Rigid Kinematic Solvers vs. Deformable Fabric Physics: Robotic articulation models (such as URDF and PartNet-Mobility) rely on rigid-body kinematic trees connected by discrete revolute or prismatic joints. Bags, jackets, and clothing represent continuum deformable surfaces whose degrees of freedom are effectively infinite. Mapping fabric deformation into discrete joint trees introduces structural approximation errors during complex panel folding.Pose Embedding Attenuation in Diffusion Transformers: Recent empirical evaluations reveal that 12-parameter extrinsic camera matrices and low-dimensional pose vectors—which conditioning modules successfully injected into U-Net architectures like MotionCtrl—lose conditioning strength when applied to Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbones due to higher transformer embedding dimensions. Conditioning DiT architectures on fine bimanual dexterity without visual artifacts remains an open challenge.Empirical LimitationsMonocular Tactile Ambiguity: Monocular RGB video contains no direct force or tactile measurements. While optimization algorithms like DiffContact enforce surface penetration bounds within $2\text{ mm}$, true grip stability depends on internal friction coefficients and material stiffness, which cannot be measured directly from monocular video without strong structural priors.3DGS Computational Overhead: Real-time video compilation using 3D Gaussian Splatting mobility parameters offers high articulation control, but requires substantial per-scene optimization time, rendering it difficult to deploy in low-latency interactive video generation pipelines.Unanswered Research QuestionsLatent Topological Operators: Can latent diffusion architectures be designed with native topological operators that execute explicit mesh-splitting primitives without requiring dense external depth or keyframe guidance?Unified Fabric-Kinematic Representations: How can CPCS unhook from rigid kinematic trees (PartNet-Mobility) and integrate continuum cloth simulation models into a single lightweight execution carrier suitable for real-time video diffusion conditioning?Generalization to Unseen Deformable Closures: How effectively does the proposed sling bag schema generalize to complex multi-stage closures, such as roll-top dry bags, drawstrings, or origami folding boxes, where material deformation and topological containment are tightly coupled?
